# Conflicts of interest disclosure and recusal procedure

## Purpose and distinction
Support two related but distinct obligations: written disclosure of potential conflicts to the Federal agency or pass-through entity under the relevant agency policy, and written standards of conduct for federally funded procurement. The organization must assess both actual and apparent conflicts. This draft does not replace board, employment, agency, or research conflict rules.

## Procedure
1. **Identify the decision.** Record the award, procurement or other relevant activity, proposed vendor or counterparty, and participating employees, officers, agents, and board members.
2. **Obtain disclosures.** Ask decision-makers to disclose financial interests, personal benefits, family or partner relationships, relevant current or prospective employment interests, and other relationships affecting impartiality.
3. **Apply procurement standards.** A person with a real or apparent conflict must not participate in selection, award, or administration of the affected federally supported contract. Follow the organization's written standards on gifts, nominal value exceptions where established, disciplinary measures, and organizational affiliate relationships.
4. **Assess award reporting.** Determine whether a written disclosure to the federal agency or pass-through entity is required under its conflict policy. Document actual submission or rationale for no submission. An internal disclosure does not necessarily meet an external reporting requirement.
5. **Resolve and monitor.** Document recusal, independent reassignment, procurement reconsideration where appropriate, retention and follow-up. Do not treat management approval as permission for prohibited conflicted participation.

## Privacy and oversight
Keep identifiable disclosures in approved restricted systems. Public issues and examples must exclude real individuals and identifiable vendor relationships. Apply stronger institutional standards if required. If a parent, affiliate, or subsidiary relationship is present, assess organizational conflicts under section 200.318(c)(2).

## Primary references
[2 CFR 200.112](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.112) (agency conflict disclosures) and [200.318(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.318) (procurement conduct and organizational conflicts). This example suggests a documentation workflow, not a new categorical federal reporting rule.
