# Personnel cost documentation and reconciliation

## Purpose and scope
A suggested control for labor charged to federal awards. It is not a mandatory timesheet format or a substitute for the recipient's payroll, compensation, labor law, and grants administration requirements. Use applicable award-specific salary limits and fringe benefit treatment.

## Procedure
1. **Establish the basis.** Record appointment or employment information, compensation rate, approved work scope, award and nonaward cost objectives, payroll period, and any applicable salary restrictions.
2. **Support work performed.** Maintain records that accurately reflect activity performed, cover the employee's total compensated activities (not over 100%), are incorporated into official records, and distinguish federal and nonfederal work as required. Documentation systems vary; do not assume monthly certifications are universally required.
3. **Reconcile payroll allocations.** Compare amounts charged with source payroll records, supported activity distributions, period-of-performance restrictions, and approved adjustments.
4. **Review estimates.** Budget projections alone do not substantiate final charges. If estimates support interim charging, perform periodic after-the-fact reviews under effective internal controls, record significant changes, and make required adjustments.
5. **Review exception cases.** Apply additional requirements for nonexempt employee hours, IHE institutional base salary, cost sharing, supplemental pay, or negotiated alternative methods where applicable.
6. **Approve and retain.** Document preparer, review date, exceptions, corrections, posting reference, and confidential storage location.

## Worked check (synthetic)
An employee's documented compensated activity is 60% Award A, 25% Award B, and 15% institutional work, totaling 100%. On a hypothetical $8,000 period salary, tentative allocations are $4,800, $2,000, and $1,200 respectively. This arithmetic is **not** a finding that the salaries are allowable, or that a percentage allocation by itself satisfies required supporting-record standards. Review actual record reliability, applicable caps, classification, award scope, and payroll reconciliation before charging.
