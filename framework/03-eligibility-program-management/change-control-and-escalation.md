# Program change-control and escalation register

## Purpose
Prevent unapproved changes to an approved project, scope, budget or key staff from being treated as routine internal plan edits. Some deviations require written federal agency or pass-through approval **even when the total budget is unchanged**.

## Workflow
1. Describe the proposed change, business reason, affected award, current authorized plan or term, proposed effective date and responsible official.
2. Check whether the change affects scope, objectives, named key personnel, principal investigator time, participant support categories, subaward activities, cost sharing, funding, construction versus nonconstruction, or period of performance.
3. Apply the specific prior-approval triggers in [2 CFR 200.308(f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.308), plus actual award and agency requirements. For example, a change in approved scope or objective requires a request for prior written approval even without an associated budget revision. Do not assume every budget movement requires approval.
4. Hold implementation where prior written approval is required until approval is actually received. An internal approval, submitted request, unanswered email, or informal conversation is not a federal approval.
5. Record determination, request, transmission date, written decision, conditions, budget or scope version, and effect on indicators and reporting.
6. Update the operating workplan, authorized budget and affected files only after permitted changes are established. Track unresolved variances through an action log.

## Escalation examples
- Change in an approved project's scope or objective: review prior written approval under 200.308(f)(1), regardless of budget impact.
- Project director's disengagement for more than three months or a 25% reduction in committed time and effort over the course of the period of performance: examine 200.308(f)(3) and award terms.
- Subaward activities not proposed and approved in the federal award: evaluate 200.308(f)(6). Different requirements apply to replacing an already-approved subrecipient. Do not confuse procurement contracts with subawards.
- Requests to extend the project period: evaluate section 200.308(f)(10), any authorized one-time extension in (g)(2), and applicable agency instructions rather than assuming unilateral extension authority.
- Issues involving credible evidence of potentially reportable misconduct need appropriate organizational escalation and legal review under [2 CFR 200.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.113). A generic action log does not substitute for required external disclosures.
