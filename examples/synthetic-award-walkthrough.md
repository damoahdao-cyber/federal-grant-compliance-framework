# Three synthetic financial management examples

Version: `0.1.0-alpha`. All figures are fictional and correspond to the seeded [starter workbook](../framework/01-financial-management/tools/financial-management-starter.xlsx).

Read the [workbook instructions](../framework/01-financial-management/workbook-guide.md) first. Use a copy of the workbook and leave the original examples available for comparison. The schedules are independent. Values do not flow automatically between them.

## 1. Review an award budget and a portfolio exception

### Prepare the review

1. Open **Budget review**. The example approved period runs January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027, with a September 30, 2026 review cutoff.
2. In a real review, record the award identifiers, authorized budget version, preparer, reviewer, and source documents in the [award register](../framework/01-financial-management/award-register.md).
3. Reconcile actual expenditures to the ledger for the same scope. Enter open obligations without counting them again in uncommitted forecast costs. The example considers the federal share only.

### Check the seeded totals

| Measure | Amount | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Approved budget | $200,000 | Authorized federal-share budget for this schedule |
| Actual expenditures | $50,000 | Posted expenditures through the cutoff |
| Open obligations | $20,000 | Committed amounts not already in actuals |
| Additional uncommitted forecast | $114,500 | Expected remaining costs excluding actuals and obligations |
| Balance after obligations | $130,000 | $200,000 - $50,000 - $20,000 |
| Estimated total cost | $184,500 | $50,000 + $20,000 + $114,500 |
| Forecast headroom | $15,500 | $200,000 - $184,500 |
| Actual budget used | 25% | $50,000 / $200,000 |

The $130,000 balance is not an amount to draw immediately. The $15,500 forecast headroom depends on the completeness of the forecast and does not authorize rebudgeting or spending. Apply the [budget monitoring guide](../framework/01-financial-management/budget-monitoring.md) and check award-specific prior approval requirements.

### Investigate the portfolio exception

4. Open **Portfolio**. The three seeded awards total $430,000 funding, $226,000 actual expenditures, and $36,000 obligations. Their combined balance after obligations is $168,000. Estimated total cost is $414,500 and forecast headroom is $15,500.
5. Examine **SYN-003** as displayed in the workbook. Its $80,000 funding, $76,000 actuals, and $6,000 obligations produce a negative $2,000 balance. Its $85,000 estimated total cost produces negative $5,000 forecast headroom.
6. Record the exception, verify input accuracy, assess obligations and allowable corrective options, assign an owner, and document review. Positive headroom on other awards does not authorize shifting costs to them.
7. Use the [award monitoring checklist](../framework/01-financial-management/award-monitoring-checklist.md) to document follow-up. For an actual financial report, separately complete the [financial reporting checklist](../framework/01-financial-management/financial-reporting-checklist.md) and applicable report form.

Expected learning result: distinguish available budget, forecast cost, and award-specific exceptions. No real review or compliance outcome is reported here.

## 2. Allocate a shared direct cost

1. Open **Cost allocation**. The example pool is $12,000, with documented usage units of 60, 30, and 10 for the three benefiting activities.
2. Confirm in a real application that the cost is allowable, treated consistently, benefits the included activities, and is appropriately classified as a shared direct cost. Include nonfederal beneficiaries where applicable.
3. Check the result below and retain the usage evidence and allocation rationale.

| Activity position in the example | Usage units | Share | Allocation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| First | 60 | 60% | $7,200 |
| Second | 30 | 30% | $3,600 |
| Third | 10 | 10% | $1,200 |
| Total | 100 | 100% | $12,000 |

4. Confirm the allocation total agrees to the pool and the rounding difference is zero. Have an authorized reviewer approve the basis before posting.
5. Follow the [cost allocation methodology](../framework/01-financial-management/cost-allocation-methodology.md). Do not use this schedule as an indirect cost rate model or as personnel effort certification.

Expected learning result: reproduce a proportional allocation and identify the evidence needed to support its basis. A numerically balanced allocation can still use an inappropriate basis.

## 3. Prepare SEFA working data

1. Open **SEFA preparation**. This separate example uses the fiscal year July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027. Its future-period data are entirely hypothetical. They are not derived from the September 2026 budget example.
2. For a real SEFA, identify the auditee's fiscal year, applicable expenditure basis, assistance types, and award records. Replace all fictional ALN and award identifiers.
3. Check the two seeded award rows and summary. Own direct expenditures of $106,000 exclude both indirect costs and amounts provided to subrecipients. Adding $11,000 indirect costs and $13,000 provided to subrecipients yields $130,000 federal expenditures. The subrecipient disclosure total is $13,000.
4. Reconcile the amounts to financial records. Check for duplicates, completeness, pass-through identifiers, and the appropriate expenditure treatment for any special assistance types.
5. Assemble program, agency, and cluster presentation and required notes outside this starter schedule. Follow the [SEFA preparation guide](../framework/01-financial-management/sefa-preparation.md). The workbook is a working schedule, not a complete SEFA or audit report.

Expected learning result: distinguish the component amounts, fiscal-year scope, and additional presentation requirements.

## Before replacing the examples

Record the workbook version and source dates. Complete every required numeric input, including explicit zeros where appropriate. Preserve formulas and check row limits. Investigate review messages, reconcile to source records, document exceptions, and obtain reviewer sign-off. Keep sensitive award and personnel records outside the public repository.
